\chapter{Memory, MMIO, and Resource Management}
\label{c6:ch}

{\large\itshape Where a driver gets memory, how it reaches its hardware, and how it gives both
back.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The kernel's memory map, and the difference between an address and a mapping.
  \item \code{kmalloc}, \code{vmalloc}, \code{kvmalloc}, \code{alloc_pages}: four allocators and how
    to pick.
  \item GFP flags as a statement about context, not about urgency.
  \item \code{request_mem_region}, \file{/proc/iomem}, and what claiming a region does and does not
    prevent.
  \item \code{ioremap} and \code{__iomem}: why a register is not memory and must not be treated as
    such.
  \item \code{readl} and \code{writel}, ordering, and when \code{_relaxed} is safe.
  \item \code{devm_*}, the error path it deletes, and why you cannot use it yet.
  \item Map \code{qa-dev}, read its identity, and prove the mapping is right.
  \item The DMA API, for the device that reads memory itself; there is nothing to run, because
    \code{qa-dev} cannot do DMA.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

This chapter is about the two things a driver needs that its callbacks do not give it: memory, and
a way to reach its hardware. It explains the difference between a physical and a virtual address,
which of the four allocators to ask and what each of them fails like, and why the GFP flag is a
statement about the context you are in rather than about how badly you want the memory. It then
follows one device from the numbers in its device tree node to a register read: claiming the
region, mapping it, reading it with accessors the compiler cannot rearrange, and unwinding all of
it in reverse when something fails. The unwinding is written by hand, because \code{devm_} needs a
device and a bare module does not have one.

Read \secref{c6:app:a} first; it is kernel memory and the allocators. Its \secref{c6:app:a6}, the
DMA API, is reading only: \code{qa-dev} cannot do DMA, so nothing in it is measured or tested, and
the section says what that costs. \Secref{c6:app:b} is MMIO and resource management, and prints the
device's register map, which every driver from here on is written against. The lab is specified in
Exercise~\ref{c6:ex:7}: a module, \code{qa_mmio}, that takes the device's physical address as a
parameter, claims and maps the region, checks the identity register, and writes and reads back
\code{SCRATCH}. The exercises in \secref{c6:sec:exercises} end with the Cross-check: translate
\code{qa-dev}'s \code{reg} through its parent's \code{ranges} by hand, predict what the first two
registers will read, then map them and find out. The lab is done when \code{make test L=L06}
reports \textbf{PASSED}, with nine checks.
