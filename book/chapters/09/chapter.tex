\chapter{Sleeping, Waiting, and Time}
\label{c9:ch}

{\large\itshape The half the software drives: a reader that wants data which does not exist
yet.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item What sleeping is: a task state, a queue, and a scheduler that stops choosing you.
  \item Wait queues, and the condition that is re-tested after every wake.
  \item The lost wakeup: why the obvious flag-and-sleep is wrong every time, not rarely.
  \item Completions, and when they are the simpler answer.
  \item Blocking \code{read()}, \code{O_NONBLOCK}, and \code{-EAGAIN} at the right moment.
  \item \code{poll}, and what a driver must implement for \code{select} to work on it.
  \item Signals, \code{-ERESTARTSYS}, and an interruptible sleep that gets interrupted.
  \item Jiffies, \code{HZ}, \code{ktime}; delaying against sleeping; timers against hrtimers.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

This chapter is the other half of Chapter~\ref{c8:ch}: a program has asked for data that does not
exist yet, and something has to happen to it in the meantime. It explains what sleeping is, a task
state, a queue, and a scheduler that stops choosing you, and why the obvious test-then-sleep loses
its wakeup; what \code{wait_event_interruptible} expands to, and which line of it closes the window;
how a blocking \code{read()} must answer \code{O_NONBLOCK} and a signal; and what \code{poll} buys.
Then it turns to time: jiffies and \code{HZ}, \code{ktime} for measuring, delaying against
sleeping, timers against hrtimers, and why \code{msleep(1)} on this course's kernel takes eight
milliseconds.

Read \secref{c9:app:a} first; it is sleeping and wait queues. \Secref{c9:app:b} is time, delays and
timers, with the course's measurement of what each kind of sleep actually costs. The lab is
specified by Exercises~\ref{c9:ex:6} and~\ref{c9:ex:7}: the driver from Chapter~\ref{c8:ch} with a
character device added, whose \code{read} blocks until the interrupt has data for it and which
implements \code{poll}, checked by the userspace tester \code{qa_wait_test} that ships with the
course; and a module that measures the sleeps. The exercises in \secref{c9:sec:exercises} end with
the Cross-check: predict what \code{msleep(1)}, \code{msleep(4)} and \code{msleep(10)} cost given
\code{HZ}, then measure them. One call takes eight times what it asks for, and two of the three
answers are the same number. The lab is done when \code{make test L=L09} reports \textbf{PASSED},
including the \code{poll} test driven from userspace.
