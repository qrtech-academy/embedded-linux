\chapter{Interrupts and Deferred Work}
\label{c8:ch}

{\large\itshape The half of the mechanism the hardware drives: a line goes high, and whatever was
running stops.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The path from a wire going high to your function being called, and the three numbers along
    it.
  \item \file{/proc/interrupts}, and what each column is actually counting.
  \item \code{request_irq}, the handler signature, and \code{IRQ_HANDLED} against \code{IRQ_NONE}.
  \item Level and edge triggering, and an acknowledge that has to happen in the right order.
  \item What a handler may not do, and what happens when it does it anyway.
  \item Four places to put the work that does not belong in the handler.
  \item Threaded handlers, and why they are the default answer now.
  \item Take \code{qa-dev}'s interrupt, acknowledge it correctly, and move the work out.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

This chapter is about the half of a driver the hardware drives: a device raises a line, and whatever
the CPU was doing stops while your function runs. It follows an interrupt from the device's wire to
your handler, through the three numbers it has on the way and the tool that prints each, and shows
what \file{/proc/interrupts} is counting. It explains \code{request_irq} and what a handler owes the
system: \code{IRQ_NONE} when the interrupt is not its own, and an acknowledgement of a
level-triggered device in the right order, without which the machine never runs anything else
again. It derives what a handler may not do from one fact, that it runs on time borrowed from
whatever it interrupted, and then gives the four places to put the work that does not belong in it,
and the reason the threaded handler is the default answer now.

Read \secref{c8:app:a} first; it is the interrupt path and the rules for the handler.
\Secref{c8:app:b} is deferred work in its four forms, with the course's measurement of a threaded
handler against a hard one. The lab is specified by Exercises~\ref{c8:ex:6} and~\ref{c8:ex:7}: a
module that finds \code{qa-dev}'s interrupt in the device tree, takes it, acknowledges it, and moves
the drain into a threaded handler, checked on the target by \file{lectures/L08/lab/run.sh}. The
exercises in \secref{c8:sec:exercises} end with the Cross-check: predict how many interrupts
\code{qa-dev} raises in two seconds from \code{PERIOD_NS}, read the delta out of
\file{/proc/interrupts}, and account for the shortfall. Your first explanation will be partly right,
and the exercise is about finding out which part. The lab is done when \code{make test L=L08}
reports \textbf{PASSED}.
