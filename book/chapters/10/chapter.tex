\chapter{The Device Model and the Device Tree}
\label{c10:ch}

{\large\itshape How a driver finds its hardware without being told where it is.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Bus, device and driver, and the match that puts the three together.
  \item kobjects and sysfs, and the reference counting under a device's lifetime.
  \item \code{probe} and \code{remove}: what may be done in each, and what must not.
  \item The platform bus, and what ``platform device'' really means.
  \item \code{of_match_table}, module aliases, and how autoloading actually happens.
  \item Device tree syntax: \code{compatible}, \code{reg}, \code{interrupts}, phandles,
    \code{\#address-cells}, \code{ranges}.
  \item Bindings, dt-schema, and where the DTB enters the boot flow.
  \item Delete every address from your driver and make it probe anyway.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

This chapter is about how a driver finds its hardware without containing a single address. It
explains the device model's three parts, the bus, the device and the driver, and the match that
joins them; what \code{probe} and \code{remove} may do, and why everything taken with \code{devm_}
is released when the device is unbound rather than when the module goes; and the four steps that
turn a device appearing into a module loading. It then reads the device tree that describes the
hardware: \code{compatible}, \code{reg} and the cells its parent declares, the translation through
\code{ranges} that Chapter~\ref{c6:ch} did by hand, and an interrupt specifier that only its
controller can decode.

Read \secref{c10:app:a} first; it is the device model. \Secref{c10:app:b} is the device tree, and
ends where the driver takes its address and its interrupt out of it. The lab is specified in
Exercise~\ref{c10:ex:6}: the Chapter~\ref{c8:ch} driver rewritten as a platform driver,
\file{qa_platform.c}, with no address and no interrupt number anywhere in its source; and
Exercise~\ref{c10:ex:7} unbinds and rebinds it to show what \code{devm_} is attached to. The
exercises in \secref{c10:sec:exercises} end with the Cross-check: work out the device's physical
address and interrupt by hand from the raw bytes of the device tree, then compare them with the
name the kernel gave the device and with what \code{probe} was handed. The lab is done when
\code{make test L=L10} reports \textbf{PASSED}, with nineteen checks.
